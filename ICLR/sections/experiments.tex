\section{Experimental Design}

\paragraph{Platforms and tasks.}
Three platforms with different policy classes and scales.
(1) RoboCasa kitchen tasks \citep{nasiriany2024robocasa} with the public GR00T
N1.5 3B multitask checkpoint, nothing trained by us: OpenCabinet, OpenDrawer,
PickPlaceCounterToCabinet and TurnOnSinkFaucet, with 102{,}348 labeled stamps
from 800 human demonstrations. We also train ACT policies
\citep{zhao2023act} on the same demonstrations as a second policy class.
(2) LIBERO-Long \citep{liu2023libero} with per-task diffusion policies
\citep{chi2023diffusion}.
(3) MimicGen \citep{mandlekar2023mimicgen} and robomimic tasks with diffusion
policies. Every evaluation cell is 50 episodes, and every switching evaluation
includes the fully closed-loop branch $(k_s{=}16, k_u{=}1)$.

\paragraph{Noise floor.}
Success rates on 50 episodes carry a sampling standard error near 7 points, so
the difference between two independent 50-episode runs has a standard error
near 10 points. We measure this directly rather than assuming it. Pairs of runs
that execute identical behavior, a fixed-16 run and a shadow run that scores
the signal without acting on it, differ by 6, 8, 18 and 4 points on the four
RoboCasa tasks, and two seeds of one identical switching configuration differ
by 6 points. We therefore treat differences below about 10 points on a single
pair of cells as unresolved, and say so wherever it applies.

\paragraph{Experiments per claim.}
For Claim 1, offline labeling of all datasets, a probe-size sweep at
$\sigma_u \in \{0.025, 0.05, 0.10\}$, and online shadow runs that score every
replan of a fixed-16 policy without acting, which give the regime distribution
on the states the policy actually visits.
For Claim 2, predictor training on held-out demonstrations with three seeds per
configuration, a privileged contact-flag baseline, cross-task transfer, and a
comparison of competing replan signals measured at the same states.
For Claim 3, fixed-$k$ sweeps, oracle switching, learned switching with h1 and
h4, a rate-matched random control that fires at the same rate but at random
replans, and a paired fork test that removes episode variance by running both
decisions from one simulator state.
